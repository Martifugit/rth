# Boss concepts: briefing (2026-11-01)

*What you're looking at in the Blender scene (the `Bosses` collection, rows from y = 150) and why. A
creative exploration, not decisions: the point is to find out what kind of boss works for R'th.*

## How to read the board

- Each cell is one boss as a **maquette**: simple shapes in roughly the game's style, built to judge the
  idea and the silhouette, not the modelling. A label stands in front; the small indigo figure is the
  player (1.8 m).
- Look at each from the game camera (above and behind, about 40°) as well as close up: the test is
  whether you can tell what it is **in one second**.

## What every concept had to meet

Drawn from what makes bosses memorable (a readable silhouette, one standout feature, a threat the shape
telegraphs, a creature that seems to belong to its world) and from your notes:

1. **A real thing made slightly fantastical.** An animal, a person, an object, a myth: one step from
   the real, so it is understood at once.
2. **One standout thing that sets the theme.** Everything else serves it.
3. **Its own place in R'th.** Tied to a thread of `docs/lore.md` or a region, so it couldn't be dropped
   into any other game unchanged.
4. **A reveal.** How the player meets it: most of these are not seen whole at first.
5. **A fight that comes from the creature.** One or two mechanics that are what the creature *is*.

## The process

- **Long list:** 34 raw ideas (real animals, myths, lore threads).
- **Cut (10):** a crab (rejected earlier), a generic giant worm, a lamprey pit (a worse Lure), the glass
  stag (a third moon beast, weaker than the other two), a headless rider (fits any game), a siege snail
  (charming, nothing of R'th in it), a giant moth (too close to the Moon Jelly), a hydra (formulaic), a
  wolf king (we have wolves), a mimic chest (a joke, not a boss).
- **Each survivor written up, then critiqued and revised.** The "Revised" line under each says what the
  second pass changed and why.

## The concepts

### 1. The Lure
- **From:** the deep-sea anglerfish. **Thread:** the cracked moon; moonfall sites.
- **The one thing:** the brightest moon shard in the crater, the one everyone came to mine, is its lure.
- **Silhouette:** a glowing shard on a long arching stalk over black water; a crescent of fangs.
- **Where:** a flooded moonfall crater. **Reveal:** you reach for the shard; the water rises into a jaw.
- **Fight:** it moves the lure; players who chase it are pulled into reach. Light the water and you see
  it; leave it dark and you only see the shard.
- **Revised:** first pass hid everything underwater, so the board showed only a stalk. Now the head
  breaks the surface as a low mound with two milky eyes, and the fangs show as a crescent of the lower
  jaw: the menace is visible before the reveal.

### 2. The Moon Jelly
- **From:** the moon jellyfish (Aurelia: four glowing rings in a clear bell). **Thread:** the moon; Candora.
- **The one thing:** at night, it looks like the moon low over the valley.
- **Silhouette:** a broad bell with four glowing rings, a curtain of fine tendrils, four frilled arms.
- **Where:** high valleys at night. **Reveal:** the "moon" is too close; then its tendrils sweep the grass.
- **Fight:** the tendrils sweep in patterns; what it has caught hangs inside the bell (earlier victims).
- **Revised:** first pass's oral arms were white spikes; now frilled ribbons, and the four rings are the
  mark that makes it a moon jelly, not a jellyfish.

### 3. The Old Tortoise
- **From:** the giant tortoise. **Thread:** the revision: places on old maps that aren't where the records
  put them.
- **The one thing:** a village that "moved" in the records moved because it is on its back.
- **Silhouette:** a domed, plated shell with roofs and a broken tower on it; a beaked head on a wrinkled neck.
- **Where:** wherever it wanders; scholars chase it. **Reveal:** the village's edge is a cliff, and the
  cliff lifts its head.
- **Fight:** on its back, among the houses, while it walks; the shell tilts.
- **Revised:** first pass carried a crater (a third moon idea) and its head read badly. Now it carries a
  moved village, which ties to the revision, and the head has a beak and neck folds.

### 4. The Heron of the Fall
- **From:** the grey heron. **Thread:** the fens of the lesser houses.
- **The one thing:** a neck far too long, coiled.
- **Silhouette:** stilt legs among reeds, a hunched body, an S of neck reaching across the marsh.
- **Reveal:** perfectly still, a shape in the reeds; it strikes from across the arena.
- **Fight:** it never moves its feet; the strike reaches anywhere; the reeds hide its legs.

### 5. The Harvestman
- **From:** the harvestman (daddy-long-legs). **Thread:** caves beneath the spine.
- **The one thing:** a small pale body hung in the middle of a vault on legs braced into every wall.
- **Reveal:** you see only legs piercing the walls; then you look up.
- **Fight:** it moves by moving its legs; the body comes down to feed.
- **Revised:** the legs now end in the walls of the vault rather than the open ground.

### 6. The Bell-Ringer
- **From:** the bell that tolled for the player (`docs/lore.md` VI). **Thread:** death; Tolling.
- **The one thing:** a gaunt robed giant whose head is a cracked bell; its clapper hangs where a jaw would be.
- **Reveal:** you hear it toll before you see it, as you heard it the night you came back.
- **Fight:** each toll freezes everyone who isn't moving; it rings for someone (marks a player).
- **Revised:** twice. First a lampshade; then a true bell profile hanging in front of the body; now the
  bell sits on its shoulders as its head, the clapper hanging below the lip like a jaw.

### 7. The Unquiet Saint
- **From:** the lore's own boss. **Thread:** the bleeding god; the church's crypts.
- **The one thing:** a martyr dragging his own reliquary on chains, a tomb three times his size.
- **Fight:** the reliquary is the weapon: he swings it on its chains; candles on it fall and burn.
- **Revised:** first pass was a gold box. Now a peaked reliquary on runners with spires and candles,
  chains from his wrists, a cracked halo.

### 8. The Collector
- **From:** a tax collector; the lore's debt: break a blood oath and p-sworn calls the blood in.
- **The one thing:** its ribs are a cage, and inside are the lights of everyone who broke an oath.
- **Fight:** it weighs players on its balance; the ledger it trails lists who owes.
- **Revised:** renamed from "Tithe-Collector": it collects oaths, not tithes.

### 9. The Fused (the betrayers)
- **From:** the lore: the first oathbreakers fused into one abomination with no faces of their own.
- **The one thing:** a mass of faceless bodies grown into one, each hand holding up a borrowed face.
- **Fight:** each mask is someone it has been; it speaks in the voice of someone you trust.
- **Revised:** first pass was one cloaked figure among floating masks (masks floating was the problem).
  Now many bodies fused, and every mask is held in a hand: the betrayers' fusion is the standout.

### 10. The Rejected Dragon
- **From:** a first draft. **Thread:** p-spoil, the revision's rejects.
- **The one thing:** one half is a finished dragon; the other half is a blank draft.
- **Fight:** its unfinished half can't be hurt the normal way (it isn't fully real).
- **Revised:** the draft half was on the side away from the camera; now it faces you: grey, flattened, a
  wing that is one flat plane and a head that is a blank block, beside a finished russet half with a bone wing.

### 11. The Bog-Giant's Hand
- **From:** bog bodies (Tollund Man), the drowned giant. **Thread:** the fens.
- **The one thing:** a hand the size of a tower rising out of the bog, an eye open in its palm.
- **Reveal:** fingertips break the surface all round you first.

### 12. The Long Pilgrim
- **From:** pilgrims on Palmerway who walk to give blood. **Thread:** Cruor's monuments.
- **The one thing:** a cowled walker on legs as tall as a steeple, a censer swinging far below it.
- **Fight:** the censer's smoke is the danger; it walks over you.

### 13. The Murder
- **From:** crows on a battlefield. **Thread:** the open war between the empire and Gall.
- **The one thing:** a giant made of crows, its edges always coming apart.
- **Fight:** it breaks into a flock and re-forms elsewhere.

### 14. The Mother-of-Spores
- **From:** the zombie-ant fungus (Cordyceps). **Thread:** Undermost.
- **The one thing:** a cap wider than a hall; from its gills hang the husks of those it took.
- **Revised:** gills under the cap and the husks as figures, so it reads as fungus, not an umbrella.

### 15. The Rat King
- **From:** the rat king (rats knotted by their tails). **Thread:** Tolling; a small boss.
- **The one thing:** a dozen rats, tails knotted into one living knot, moving as one wheel of teeth.
- **Revised:** built from our real rat now, not blobs.

### 16. The Field
- **From:** scarecrows. **Thread:** the empire's farmland.
- **The one thing:** a field full of straw figures; one of them is a little taller and was facing the
  other way a moment ago.
- **Fight:** a game of looking: it only moves when no one is facing it.
- **Revised:** scarecrows on a single pole with drooping arms and sack heads (no crossbars: no crosses).

### 17. The Candle Abbess
- **From:** votive candles; nuns. **Thread:** the church's crypts.
- **The one thing:** a nun of melting wax, her crown a ring of candles.
- **Fight:** every hit melts her; the wax pools and spreads over the floor, slowing everyone.
- **Revised:** first pass read as a chess piece; now a habit, wimple and veil with wax running down.

### 18. The Orrery
- **From:** an armillary sphere. **Thread:** the spine's surface people (measure, observe, refuse gods).
- **The one thing:** a walking observatory of brass rings round one great lens.
- **Fight:** its rings align to aim the lens; a beam where they line up.

### 19. The Willow Widow
- **From:** the weeping willow. **Thread:** grief, the river country.
- **The one thing:** its hanging boughs are long arms ending in hands.

### 20. The Drowned Choir
- **From:** drowned bells, sirens. **Thread:** the realm of the dead's dark water.
- **The one thing:** a ring of the drowned waist-deep round a sunken bell, singing.
- **Fight:** the song raises the water.

### 21. The Olm
- **From:** the olm, the pale blind cave salamander that Slovenians once believed was a baby dragon
  washed out of the caves. **Thread:** the deep beneath the spine.
- **The one thing:** the dragon's child: a vast pale blind thing in an underground lake, frilled gills
  blooming like red flowers either side of its head, legs too small for it.
- **Fight:** it is blind and hunts by sound: stand still.
- **Revised:** the gills were too small to carry it; now three large branching red gills each side.

### 22. The Antlion
- **From:** the antlion and its pit. **Thread:** Sere; water.
- **The one thing:** the arena is its pit: a funnel of sand, its jaws at the bottom.
- **Reveal:** the sand starts to slide toward the middle.
- **Fight:** everything slides toward the jaws; it flings sand to knock you down.
- **Revised:** the pit read flat; now deeper, banded light and dark down its slope, a cart sliding in, the
  remains of what it ate on the way down, two ember eyes under the jaws.

### 23. The Ember Axolotl
- **From:** the axolotl (frilled gills, a permanent smile). **Thread:** House Gall's volcanoes and fire.
- **The one thing:** its gill frills are burning: six branches of flame on a pale, smiling face.
- **Fight:** it bathes in the lava pool; the smile never changes, whatever it's doing.
- **Revised:** twice. The frills read first as candles, then as combs; now feathery branching gills (as the
  olm's) with flames licking off them, and a tail fin.

### 24. The Mara
- **From:** the mara of sleep paralysis, who sits on the sleeper's chest. **Thread:** the nightmare,
  moving from sleeper to sleeper; the Dreaming.
- **The one thing:** a long-limbed hag crouched on the chest of a giant sleeper; the arena is that
  sleeper, and you are inside the dream.
- **Fight:** if the sleeper wakes, the dream ends; the mara keeps it asleep.
- **Revised:** it read as a spider; now a thin hunched hag, knees drawn up past its shoulders, lank hair
  hanging over its face, long fingers on the sleeper's face. Still the weakest of the set.

### 25. The Keyholder
- **From:** a living lock. **Thread:** the dwarves' seals over the deep.
- **The one thing:** a blind giant chained as a lock, its chest a keyhole.
- **Fight:** what it guards isn't it; killing it opens the seal.
- **Revised:** arms chained out wide read as a crucifixion (and we keep crosses out); now it kneels, head
  bowed and blindfolded, arms dragged back by chains into the seal door, the keyhole large and lit.

## Questions to answer from the board

- Which reveal works best: hidden in the environment (Lure, Field, Bog Hand), seen from far away (Moon
  Jelly, Pilgrim), or you're standing on it (Tortoise, Mara, Antlion)?
- Real animal or lore figure: which side reads better in this style?
- Huge versus small: does the Rat King hold up as a boss next to the giants?

## Where it stands

- **Strongest reads** (one second, unmistakable): the Old Tortoise, the Lure, the Harvestman, the Heron, the
  Murder, the Olm, the Ember Axolotl, the Keyholder, the Antlion, the Mother-of-Spores, the Orrery.
- **Read, but plainer:** the Bell-Ringer, the Collector, the Fused, the Rejected Dragon, the Bog-Giant's
  Hand, the Rat King, the Field, the Drowned Choir, the Willow Widow, the Candle Abbess, the Moon Jelly.
- **Weakest:** the Mara (the idea is strong, the maquette isn't), the Long Pilgrim (hard to frame: it's
  mostly legs), the Unquiet Saint (the reliquary carries it; he doesn't).
- The maquettes are built by `tools/art/blender/boards/boss_concepts.py` (`run()`, or `run(only=[names])`).
