# Names (A2): proposals

*Proposed 2026-10-22, being picked with the owner from 2026-10-23. Nothing here is canon until it
is picked; until then the game keeps its current wording and the docs keep their `p-` placeholders.
Once names are picked, they go into `docs/world-map.md` ("Kept"), `docs/lore.md`, and the B2 text
pass.*

## What needs a name

Groups 1-5 shape how the world feels and are named first, one at a time. Groups 6-7 are named as
their areas are designed; a `p-` place shows on the world map as an unnamed mark until then.

Rejected so far: for the church, the Lien, the Surety, the Earnest and keeping the Sanguine Synod;
for the surface people, the Reckoners, the Unsworn, the Plumb and Meridians.

For each subject: three or four candidates, each with its meaning, how it sounds aloud, and notes.
The **recommended** one is first and in bold. Pronunciations are rough respellings; the stressed
syllable is in capitals.

**What the candidates aim for** (from `docs/world-map.md`): a proper name with a meaning under it;
short; easy to say and to shout in chat; humoral, early-modern and slightly uncanny; unique to R'th
rather than generic fantasy, and not borrowed from RuneScape or another well-known setting.
Overlap with ordinary English words is fine, and often the point.

**Already taken** (checked against the repo's docs, content and code): Cruor, Cistern, Corvin,
Sere, Sallow, Selenfall, Scalth, Meridian, Mordant, Umber, Undermost, Gall, Frostpeak, Faceless,
Tolling, Widdermill, Wennow, Palmerway, Hollis, Ambrose; and words already used in game content,
which the candidates avoid or flag: *Abjuration* (an ability), *hushfin* (a fish event in
`skills.md`), *Gloamwood* (a log tier), *kiln* (charcoal, `skills.md`), *alder* (a log tier),
*the Concord* (a building in Meridian's design), *Plumb Line* (in Meridian's design). Letters
already crowded: **C**, **S**, **M**, **W**. Letters still free: A, B, D, E, I, J, K, L, N, O,
Q, V, Y.

## p-sworn's church (replaces "the Sanguine Synod")

| Candidate | Meaning, origin | Aloud | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **the Lien** | A lien is a creditor's right to hold your goods until a debt is paid. p-sworn holds your blood against your oath: the church *is* its claim on you. | LEEN | Short, unusual, a legal chill that suits the "debt collector" face. Reads well on things: "Lien strongbox", "Lien-minted coin", "a clerk of the Lien", "sealed with Lien wax". L is free. |
| the Surety | A surety stands bail for another's debt; the blood is surety for the oath. | SHOOR-uh-tee | Clear meaning, but three syllables and another S. |
| the Earnest | Earnest money: a token paid to bind a bargain. Also "sincere", the church's public face. | UR-nist | A lovely double meaning, but it reads as an adjective in sentences ("an Earnest clerk"). |
| the Sanguine Synod (keep) | Blood and a council of priests. | SANG-gwin SIN-od | No text to change, but "Synod" is generic church and the name belonged to the old Tithe-era lore. |

**Recommended: the Lien.** It says what the church really does (holds what you owe) without saying
"blood", so the bleeding-god secret stays hidden behind a dry, slightly sinister word.

## The spine's surface people (replaces "the Rationalists")

| Candidate | Meaning, origin | Aloud | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **the Reckoners** | To reckon is to count and calculate. They measure everything, forecast moonfalls and shoot by the numbers, and they stand outside every god's reckoning. | REK-uh-nerz | Fits ranged and Artifice text as is ("the Reckoners' patient art of fighting from a distance", "the Reckoners call religion a fever"). A YA novel series shares the word; no game setting does. |
| the Unsworn | They swear no oath to any god. | un-SWORN | The sharpest meaning, but the dwarves won't swear either, and it sits too close to *the Untroth*. Best as what **the empire** calls them, a slur they wear proudly. |
| the Plumb | True as a plumb line: upright, measured, unbending. | PLUM | Short, but plain, and "Plumb Line" is already a place in Meridian's design. |
| Meridians | After their capital, as a nation. | meh-RID-ee-unz | No new word, but it loses the creed. |

**Recommended: the Reckoners**, with "the Unsworn" as the empire's name for them. Their realm then
needs no name of its own (see Concordia below).

## The realm of the dead

| Candidate | Meaning, origin | Aloud | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Quietus** | Death, and the discharge of a debt ("might his quietus make", Hamlet). A realm where a ferryman settles your account. | KWY-ee-tus | Ties death to the world's theme of debts and collateral. Q is free. Also the name of a discipline in *Vampire: the Masquerade* (niche). |
| the Dimmet | West-country dialect for dusk. The landing is very dark. | DIM-it | The most unique; homely and eerie at once. Runner-up. |
| the Ebb | Where the dead ebb to; a tide for a ferry. | EB | Short and clear, but plain. |
| Lowe | An old word for a burial mound (Old English *hlaw*), and also a flame. | LOW | Easily misread as "low". |

**Recommended: Quietus.** "Ferried back from Quietus" sounds right in chat, and the word's second
meaning (a debt paid) is exactly what the ferryman does.

## The walker

She (or he) tried the long way back in life and failed. Human names, because the walker was a
person.

| Candidate | Meaning, origin | Aloud | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Tamsin Fare** | *Fare* is both a journey and the fee for one. She wouldn't pay the ferry's fare, walked, and fell short; now she sees others through. | TAM-zin FAIR | Warm, sayable, and the surname carries the story. |
| Mother Wayless | *Wayless*: without a path. What the dead call her. | WAY-liss | A title and an epithet; motherly, a little eerie. |
| Hobb Shortfall | He fell short. Hob: an old name for a country fellow. | HOB SHORT-fawl | On the nose, a touch comic. |
| Ida Halfway | How far she got. | EYE-da HAF-way | Plain. |

**Recommended: Tamsin Fare.** (Avoided: "Nearly Nell" and similar, which echo Harry Potter's
Nearly Headless Nick.)

## The gods: one family

The gods and the constants should sound related. Three ways to do that:

| Family | The idea | p-sworn | p-betrayers | p-scattered | p-head | dreamer | keeper of the dead | keeper of p-spoil |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Old words (recommended)** | Short, plain English words that survive in everyday speech, as if the language remembers gods its speakers forgot. People still swear "by my troth" and tell stories "of yore" without knowing they are naming gods. | Troth | the Untroth | Sherd | Yore | Sweven | Hush | Cull |
| B. Learned Latin | The Latin of courts and apothecaries; matches Cruor, Sere and Cistern. | Jurat | the Perjured | Disjecta | Memor | Sopor | Tacet | Calx |
| C. Folk epithets | What common people call them. Better as their **faces** (each god's sheet has a Faces field) than as their names. | the Creditor | the Traitor (already in use) | the Parted | Old Brow | the Sleeper | the Warden | the Sexton |

**Recommended: family A.** It is the most R'th-like: the gods are hidden in ordinary speech, which
suits a world whose people "barely know". Their inspirations stay discoverable (troth and its
breaking, a broken pot's pieces, a memory of long ago). Family B sounds like scholarship rather
than gods anyone swears by; family C gives each god's faces.

## Where Scalth's mites and salts come from

The vitriol mites, the lye vat, the glass flensing hook and "Scalth salt" are in the game. The old
cause (the Scalding) is cut.

| Candidate | Meaning, origin | Aloud | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Scalth** (kept, rehomed) | The scalding pans on Brack's broken coast, where a lesser house boils brine and worse into salts; the vitriol mites breed in what's left. From *scald*: salt makers boiled brine in pans. Sour Hollow's cask came from there, bought quietly by someone in the empire. | SKAWLTH | No item names change; only Corvin's and Wenna's lines and a few examines. |
| Copperas | The old trade name for green vitriol; copperas works boiled it from stones on English coasts. | KOP-er-us | The strongest real link to "vitriol". |
| Bittern | The bitter liquor left in a salt pan after the salt is drawn; also a fen bird with a booming call. | BIT-ern | Two meanings, both fen. Shares B with Brack. |
| Lixwell | A lye spring (Latin *lixivium*, lye). | LIKS-well | Invented. |

**Recommended: keep Scalth**, with the new origin. It already sounds like scald and salt, and
keeping it saves text.

### p-demigod sites

Too early to name each one: the demigods aren't designed. **A pattern instead:** each piece's
place is named after an **herb of signature**, the early-modern belief that a plant shaped like a
body part heals it. That fits the humoral world, and it hides the dismembered god in plain sight:
the places are named after body parts, and nobody notices.

| Piece | Place name | Aloud |
| --- | --- | --- |
| an eye | **Eyebright** | EYE-brite |
| the lungs (the breath) | **Lungwort** | LUNG-wurt |
| the heart | **Hartshorn** (an apothecary's salt; *hart*, heart) | HARTS-horn |
| the liver | **Liverwort** | LIV-er-wurt |
| a hand | **Handsel** (a first gift, for luck) | HAND-sel |
| the teeth | **Toothwort** | TOOTH-wurt |
