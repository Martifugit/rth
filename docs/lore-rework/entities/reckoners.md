# The Reckoners

**Status:** proposed sheet. The name replaces "the Rationalists"; "the Unsworn" is the empire's name for them.

## Origin

The Reckoners formed when highland towns refused the empire's blood oath and began sharing records: rain, crop yields, moonfalls, avalanches, troop movements and failed prophecies. Their first charters say that a god may be real without being owed obedience.

## Belief

Nothing is made less wondrous by being measured. Gods, dreams and Turns are dangers precisely because they are real, so their signs must be observed rather than served. They reject service to gods, not the existence of gods.

## Ground and daily life

The Reckoners hold passes, terraces, beacon chains and the towns that provision them. Their wealth comes from tolls, precision craft, charts, guns and route knowledge. Public halls display ledgers, weather instruments and disputed predictions. Their culture prizes a claim that can be checked.

## The Concord

The **Concord** divides surface and deep: passes and sky to the Reckoners, caves to the dwarves, no questions asked across the boundary. Both parties violate the last clause. The treaty survives because both know that open war would expose what lies below.

## Wants and weakness

They want to keep the spine neutral and turn reliable knowledge into independence. Their weakness is that a method built for repeatable nature falters when a Turn changes the premises. Their leaders may begin suppressing evidence that cannot be reconciled with their models.

## Clock

Their observatories predict a moonfall that their own records say is impossible. A faction wants to publish the warning; another wants to hide it until the passes can be secured. Delay risks catastrophe and makes the empire's offered protection tempting.

## In play

- Ranged combat, surveying, signal towers, observatory puzzles and long-distance trade.
- The player can use reliable measurements to expose lies, but must decide who gets dangerous data.
- An early quest proves the Reckoners right; a later one proves their model incomplete rather than foolish.
