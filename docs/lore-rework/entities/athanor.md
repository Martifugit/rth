# The Athanor

**Status:** proposed. This is an occult source, not yet a confirmed god or major being.

## What it is believed to be

The houses call the Athanor the first furnace: a buried source through which substance can be altered. Gall says it is proof that the world wants to be remade. Lesser houses call it a wound, and the dwarves refuse to name it at all.

## What the evidence suggests

It is an ancient engine, organ, or unfinished mechanism beneath Undermost. Around its vents, matter does not merely burn: salts breed, metals remember different shapes, and humours become tools of transformation. Occult practice is disciplined exposure to these leaks.

## What is sealed

- Whether it belongs to the maker, an earlier rejected world, or a being trapped beneath this one.
- Whether it is related to the bound thing in the deep.
- What it consumes when a major working succeeds.

## Bargain

The Athanor does not speak or demand worship. It gives change for a price that is not always known until later: material, heat, bodily balance, memory, or a place's former shape.

## Surface consequences

Gall controls upper vents through furnaces and claims the resulting industry as civilization. Brack's lesser houses use contaminated coast, brine pans and sea caves where smaller seepages alter salt and flesh. The dwarves maintain old seals beneath the Concord, which is why they oppose Gall's fire near the deep.

## Clock

An old seal begins to sweat black salt. Gall reads it as an opening; the dwarves read it as a failure; the lesser houses see a chance to steal a source that has made Gall dominant.

## In play

- Alchemy with legible costs, altered biomes, living industrial hazards and excavation.
- A place affected by the Athanor should offer a useful resource and a visible wrongness in equal measure.
