# Troth

**Status:** proposed sheet. It develops the decisions in `../gods.md`; nothing here is canon until the canon pass.

## The god behind the empire

Troth is made of oaths sworn in blood. No one agrees whether it was once something else, or whether the first oath made it. What is known is that, at the beginning of the empire's history, its first followers betrayed it. They struck it, took something from it, and became the Untroth.

Troth did not die. It has been bleeding ever since.

The empire does not tell that story. Its people are taught that blood seals a promise: an oath given before a monument is made real, and a soldier who swears in blood may draw on Troth's strength. It is a faith that makes promises matter. It also makes breaking them dangerous.

Only a very few people within the Lien understand what the monuments truly do. The blood gathered there does not merely witness an oath. It keeps Troth alive.

## The Lien

The Lien is Troth's church and the government of the empire. It keeps the monuments, appoints the officials who oversee them, raises the empire's soldiers, and decides how the empire understands its own history. It is not a separate priesthood standing beside the state. In practice, it is the state.

Most people encounter the Lien through ordinary life: the monument at the centre of town, a blessing before a journey, an oath spoken when taking work or joining a levy, an official arriving to account for a bad harvest. The Lien offers order and protection, and many people sincerely value both. Its cruelty is not that it is false. Its cruelty is that it cannot ever be satisfied.

Every new settlement brought within the empire becomes a place where people can swear. Every oath gives Troth something it needs. Every tithe of blood buys it more time. This is why the empire always finds a reason to keep moving outward.

## Presence

Troth has no body in the world, at least not yet. It is felt in the taste of iron where there is no blood, in a pledge that seems heavier once it has been spoken, and in the sudden strength of someone who has sworn themselves to a cause.

The empire calls on Troth openly. Beyond its borders, people may fear it, resent the empire that serves it, or have names for it of their own; those names should come from the particular people and place rather than be fixed in this sheet.

## What it wants

Troth needs blood and it needs oaths. Without fresh blood, its wound deepens. Without new promises, it grows thin and weak. The Lien knows enough to answer both needs with expansion, enlistment, settlement and ceremony.

Troth's great fear is quieter than war: a world in which nobody swears anything at all. Its secret fear is that whatever wounded it is still somewhere in R'th.

## What is happening now

The old monuments have begun to draw more blood than they should. The Lien has not admitted this, even to much of itself. Local officials are pressed to find more willing hands, more levies, more ceremonies and more reasons for people to bind themselves.

That pressure makes the empire stronger in the short term and hollower in the long one. People flee their oaths. Others learn to forge them. The Faceless find new recruits among those who want a life beyond the promises they were forced to make.

If nobody interrupts this course, the Lien will answer a failing god with an emergency campaign of oaths and turn a contested border into a holy war.

## In the game

- Cruor's verses and melee pledges are the immediate, useful face of Troth's bargain.
- A monument can be a place of genuine refuge as well as extraction: a town may depend on it and still suffer beneath its demands.
- The player can discover that damaging a monument does not simply weaken the empire; it may worsen Troth's wound.
