# The Sherds

**Status:** proposed sheet for the scattered god and its demigods.

## Story

Sherd was torn apart long ago. The pieces did not die: each became a demigod, with a body, a local life, followers and a place that grew around it. None knows that it was once part of another whole. Scholars later name the pattern "the Sherds"; each community has its own older name for its patron.

## Structure

Do not make the demigods a uniform pantheon. Each is a complete person with a distinct bargain, local problem and reason to resist reunion. Their sites use names of signature-herbs—Eyebright, Lungwort, Hartshorn, Liverwort, Handsel and Toothwort—so the body pattern is visible only in retrospect.

## The central choice

The player gradually learns that reunion could restore a god capable of facing a later threat. It would also end the people now living as separate beings. The climax is persuasion, not collection: each Sherd needs a reason to surrender a life that has value.

## Followers

Each local cult should be ordinary before it is cosmic: irrigators at Lungwort, healers at Eyebright, debt-givers at Handsel, and so on. Their keepers preserve an intimate relationship, not a generic priesthood. They may become allies, opponents, or witnesses to the cost of reunion.

## Secret and clock

One piece is missing, perhaps in Riddance. Its absence explains why the remaining Sherds remember their former self only as pain, dreams or a shared aversion. As the new Turn begins, those symptoms intensify and two Sherds independently seek the missing piece for incompatible reasons.

## In play

- Six regional quest arcs that stand alone yet gain meaning together.
- Each region changes according to whether its Sherd remains, leaves voluntarily, or is forced.
- Reunion should be a genuine loss even if it is necessary.
