# <Area name>

*Draft (or: Approved by the owner, date). The area's paper design: written when it is next to be
built. Its seed is in [III, areas](../../3-story/index.md). Follows Baldwin's level outline, with
Ryan's level design and an online world's additions.*

## Synopsis

One paragraph: what this area is, what a player does here, and why it is worth coming to.

## Objectives

What the area is for, as two to four goals, each naming the [pillar](../../1-overview/pillars.md) it
serves. Then what draws players early, mid and late.

## Introductory material

How a player first arrives: by which road, sent by whom, what they see first and from how far. The
hook: the first thing that grabs them, and what it makes them want to do.

## Physical description

The land (ground, water, plants, how open or closed), what is built and how old it looks, the light
and weather, what you hear. What tells a player they are here and not next door.

## Map

The map, to scale, regenerated from the plan data. Then each place on it, one paragraph each: what
it is, why it is here, what is in it.

## Critical path

The way most players go through the area the first time: the beats in order, from arriving to
leaving, each with where it happens, what happens and what pulls the player on. Then the optional
branches off it.

## Encounters

Everything the player meets, as tables:
- **People:** who, where, what they offer or ask.
- **Creatures:** what, how many, where, how dangerous (rated), why they live here.
- **Resources:** for every skill, what can be gathered or made here, how much, where, at what levels,
  and why it belongs to this land. A skill with nothing here says so.
- **Services:** banks, shops, altars, travel.
- **Discoveries:** what can be found that no one points at.

## Walkthrough

One per stage the area serves: what a player does here, in order, as they would play it; where they
go next and why they come back.

## Closing material

How the area sends players on: the roads and errands out, what is seen of the next area from here,
and how the area changes once its quests are done.

## Connections

Each neighbouring area: what crosses the border (roads, rivers, views, errands), and the step in
difficulty between them.

## Assets

What has to be made for it: buildings, landmarks, props, creatures, sounds.

## Open questions

Numbered, for the owner.
