# How the story unfolds

*Draft (2026-11-03). From `docs/lore.md` VI, `docs/quests/the-bell.md`, the Dreamless quest and
`docs/areas/tolling-plan.md` (whose mid quest lines are proposals).*

The player is unaligned, and the game never forces a single loyalty: each power's stories show the
others from inside, and most players will do something with all of them. The story is found, not
staged: there is no opening scene, and a player can ignore every thread and go mining.

## What is designed

1. **The Bell** (the first quest): who the player is, as far as Tolling knows. It opens the church
   (the ledger), the occult (Corvin's curiosity) and the Dreaming (Edda), and points at the ferryman.
   It ends with more questions than it started with.
2. **Dreamless:** Edda's sleepwalking; Corvin brews her a dreamless draught; she says something she
   shouldn't know. It touches the nightmare thread and Corvin's secret.
3. **The first death:** Pennick already knows the player.
4. **Someone comes looking** (later, elsewhere): not someone who knew the player or wishes them well,
   but someone who knows a little more than they do, and gives it out in pieces.

## Proposed, not decided

- Tolling's people return in longer quest lines: Corvin's (the cellar and the house of Sallow),
  Edda's (the Dreaming), the bell's (why it rang; the ledger; why the player woke in the dead-house).
  The owner's idea for the last: the ferryman found it funny to have them wake there, because it made
  them come back to him with interesting stories.
- The bell's twin hangs in a drowned belfry in Knellmere: the first pull east.

## Scenes to build towards

From the canon (`docs/lore-rework/gods.md`, `untroth.md`):
- The player meets three of the Faceless in a room, and one of them isn't really there.
- The mirrored face: one of the Untroth wears a face the wrong way round (the parting, the scar, the
  ring on the other side); players can learn to notice.
- The keepers' three rules carved on old monuments, ignored by the church.
- The Untroth's body, late: three heads, one holding the heart, the other two fighting their own ways.
- The demigods' climax: persuading them to recombine, at the cost of the lives they have lived.
