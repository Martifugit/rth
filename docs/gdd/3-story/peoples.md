# Peoples and powers

*Draft (2026-11-03). From `docs/lore.md` IV.*

| Power | What it holds | Why it can hold it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cruor's empire** | The rich heart of the continent, about a third of the land, round its capital Cruor: rivers, farmland, roads, forests and a great lake. | The largest army: soldiers sworn in blood and made strong by p-sworn. |
| **House Gall** | A kingdom of its own in the south-east: volcanic, openly occult. | Fire. The largest and loudest of the occult houses, and burns first. |
| **The lesser occult houses** | Fens, sea caves and broken country in the north-east, and hidden cells in the empire's own cities. | Ground the empire can burn but not keep. |
| **The spine's surface people** | The passes, the high valleys and the observatories along the spine, from Meridian. | The high ground and the passes: every crossing pays. |
| **The dwarves** | The caves of the spine, trading from Frostpeak Forge. | Nobody else can find their way inside the mountains. |
| **The older peoples of Undermost** | The depths beneath the dwarves. | Nobody visits on purpose. |
| **Sere** | Dry country in the spine's rain shadow, from Cistern on the deepest wells. | Only its people know the water. Armies that march in die of thirst. |
| **The Faceless** | No land: hidden among every people, strongest inside the empire. | Nobody knows who they are. |

**The empire must keep growing,** because its god starves without new oaths and bleeds without new
blood. Its border is ragged: it reaches up Sere's river, into the spine's foothills, a fortress pocket
inside Gall's land and a coastal enclave in the lesser houses' fens. Each is a disputed place that can
change hands.

**The occultists** practise arts of bile, which make them terribly strong and slowly strange. Where
their power comes from is open. Gall dominates; the lesser houses include Umber, Mordant and Sallow,
the last fallen on hard times.

**The surface people** don't deny the gods, which would be absurd here: **they refuse to serve them**.
They and the dwarves share the spine under **a treaty**: the surface to one, the caves to the other,
and no questions asked in either direction. Both break the last clause.

## How the powers stand

| Between | |
| --- | --- |
| Empire and Gall | Open war along a long front. Neither can win; border towns change hands. |
| Empire and the lesser houses | It hunts them and burns their cells; some imperial lords quietly buy what they make. |
| Empire and the surface people | It needs their passes and pays their tolls. They won't swear, so it can't absorb them peacefully. |
| Empire and the dwarves | Grain for metal. The dwarves won't swear either, and the empire wants their mines. |
| Empire and Sere | Its salient up Sere's river is a hand on Sere's water; Sere pays to keep it open. |
| Empire and the Faceless | It hunts them as the Traitor's people. They hollow it out from inside. |
| Gall and the lesser houses | Gall wants them under its rule. Some submit; some hide from Gall as hard as from the empire. |
| Gall and the surface people | They sell to both sides, and profit from the war. |
| Gall and the dwarves | The dwarves keep Gall's fire away from the deep, and won't say why. |
| Gall and Sere | Gall offers alliance against the empire. Sere hedges. |
| The surface people and the dwarves | The treaty. |
| The surface people and Sere | Sere's caravans cross the spine by their passes. |
| The dwarves and Sere | Sere buys dwarven tools to dig for water. |
