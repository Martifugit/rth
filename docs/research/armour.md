# Research: kinds of armour

Status: first pass (2026-10-24). A survey of historical helmets and body armour, gathered to
design R'th's smithed plate (Smithing makes helm, body, legs, gauntlets, boots and a shield:
`docs/skills.md`). It is a menu of shapes and the reasons they read, not a style decision. The
owner picks; the chosen rules go into `tools/art/blender/README.md` once a piece has passed.

Old School RuneScape is the style target (low poly, big masses, a few flat colours), never a source
of designs. The first "iconic" set copied the rune set's layout piece for piece (dark pauldrons on a
light chest, a pointed hem, ball knee caps, a visor box on a dome) and was rightly rejected as a bad
copy. Real armour history is a much deeper well of shapes that are nobody's property.

## 1. Full (closed) helms

The face is covered; the eye opening is the helm's face. Ordered roughly by date.

| Helm | When, where | Silhouette | The one feature that reads |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Enclosed helmet / great helm** | Late 12th–14th c., Europe | A flat-topped cylinder over the whole head, eye slits, breathing holes, a neck guard | The flat top and the box: a tin can. Slits are often in the arms of a cross plate on the face |
| **Sugarloaf** | Late 13th–14th c. | The great helm with a tall conical crown (named after sugar moulds) | The cone: blows glance off it |
| **Frog-mouth (Stechhelm)** | c. 1400–1525, Germany; jousting only | Plates slope back and flare out to a slit at the top, the lower face jutting like a beak; bolted to the breastplate | The beak and the slit high in it: the wearer looks out by leaning forward |
| **Bascinet with a hounskull visor ("pig-face")** | c. 1380–1410 | A conical skull with a visor drawn out into a point like a muzzle | The snout, full of breaths. Later visors rounded off (an "ape-like" profile by 1435) |
| **Great bascinet** | 15th c. | The bascinet grown down to the shoulders, a fixed bevor | One rounded mass from crown to collar |
| **Armet** | 15th c., Italy, France, England | A round one-piece skull, hinged cheek plates locking at the chin, a visor, a short tail | Close-fitting: the head's own shape in steel |
| **Close helmet** | 15th–16th c. | The armet's successor: fully enclosed, visor and bevor pivoting at the temples, plate at the neck | The stack of visor, upper bevor and bevor, all on one pivot |
| **Close burgonet** | 16th c. | A burgonet's peak and crest with a close helmet's hinged bevor | A peak over the face and a keel-like comb |
| **Sallet with a bevor** | Mid-15th c.; German sallets have a long tail | A rounded skull with a long tail swept over the neck; a bevor rises to meet it in front, the slit between | The tail: a long sweep back, the head ending in a point behind |
| **Barbute** | 15th c., Italy | A sallet-like bowl down to the neck, after Classical Corinthian helms | The T- or Y-shaped opening for the eyes and mouth |
| **Corinthian** | 7th–6th c. BC, Greece; one bronze sheet | A bowl to the jaw with long cheek pieces, a nasal, an almond-shaped eye opening | The T-shaped face opening between the cheek pieces; a crest on top was common |
| **Vendel / spectacle helmets** | 6th–8th c., Scandinavia | Bands (round the head, front to back, ear to ear) with plates between, a face guard like spectacles | The spectacles over the eyes, the band frame |
| **Kabuto with a menpō** | Japan | A bowl, a flared neck guard of lames (shikoro), wings at the brim (fukigaeshi), a face mask, a crest | The crest (maedate): horns, moons, leaves. "Kawari kabuto" is the class of any unusual shape |

## 2. Open helms

Not full helms, but they show what a helm's silhouette can be: the brim, the peak, the comb.

| Helm | When, where | What reads |
|---|---|---|
| **Spangenhelm** | 4th–11th c. | A cone of 3–6 plates on strips meeting at a point; nasal, cheek flaps, mail |
| **Nasal helmet** | 9th–13th c. (the Normans) | A cone and a bar down the nose |
| **Montefortino** | Celtic, then Roman | A bowl with a knob on top and cheek pieces |
| **Kettle hat** | 12th–16th c., common soldiers | A dome with a wide brim: an upturned bowl |
| **Burgonet** | 16th c., cavalry | A peak over the eyes and a keel-like crest |
| **Morion** | 16th c., infantry | A tall comb and a brim curving up to points front and back: a boat |
| **Cabasset** | 16th c., Spain | A tall almond-shaped bowl, often with a small stalk at the top, and a narrow brim |
| **Chichak / lobster-tailed pot (zischägge)** | Ottoman 16th c.; Europe from c. 1600 | A conical (Ottoman) or round (European) skull, a peak, a sliding nasal, cheek pieces, a neck guard of lames; often fluted |

## 3. Body armour, by how it is built

| Kind | What it is | How it reads |
|---|---|---|
| **Mail** | Interlinked rings: hauberk (shirt), coif (hood), chausses (leggings) | Soft and cloth-like: it follows the body. Its texture carries it |
| **Scale** | Small plates overlapping like fish scales on a backing | A textured shell |
| **Lamellar** | Small plates laced into strips (Assyria onward; steppe; Japan) | Horizontal bands of texture; stiff, boxy |
| **Laminar** | Horizontal bands of solid plate, riveted to straps; replaced lamellar by c. 1500 | Rings of plate. The Roman lorica segmentata is the famous one: overlapping hoops round the torso and over the shoulders |
| **Brigandine / coat of plates** | Plates riveted inside cloth (the coat of plates has larger plates) | A padded coat studded with rivet heads: clothing that is armour |
| **Plate cuirass** | Breast and back plates joined at the shoulders and waist (14th–17th c.); a plackart over the belly | One rigid shell, a waist, often a ridge |
| **Gothic (German)** | 1420s–1500: full plate with movable joints, slender, pointed, ribbed and cusped edges, long pointed sabatons | Tall, spiky, fluted edges |
| **Milanese (Italian)** | The same period's Italian "white armour" | Rounded, smooth, broad; big asymmetric pauldrons |
| **Maximilian** | c. 1515–25: Italian roundness with German fluting over the whole surface, broad square-toed sabatons | Ridged all over, like pleated cloth in steel |
| **Ō-yoroi / dō-maru / tōsei gusoku** | Japan: laced lamellar boxes, then hinged cuirasses, then riveted plate for the gun era | Big flat shoulder boards (sode), a skirt of lames (kusazuri), a box-like trunk |

## 4. The pieces of a harness

The names used in the model scripts:

- **Neck:** gorget (plate collar), bevor (chin and throat, worn with a sallet), aventail (mail
  curtain from the helm).
- **Torso:** cuirass (breast and back), plackart (extra plate over the belly), fauld (lames below
  the waist), culet (lames over the lower back), tassets (plates hanging from the fauld over the
  thighs).
- **Arms:** pauldron (shoulder, over the armpit; Italian ones big and asymmetric), spaulder (bands
  over the shoulder, not the armpit), rerebrace (upper arm), couter (elbow), vambrace (forearm),
  gauntlet; besagew (a disc over the armpit).
- **Legs:** cuisse (thigh), poleyn (knee, with a fan-shaped wing outside), greave (shin), sabaton
  (foot).
- **Oddities:** ailettes (small heraldic boards standing on the shoulders, 1275–1350), rondels (any
  disc), grand guard (an extra jousting plate on the left).

## 5. What reads in a game, at a distance

From the game-art sources and from our own renders:

- **Silhouette first.** Block the big shapes in zoomed out; detail everywhere is as good as no
  detail. Small details should add to the silhouette, never break it.
- **Shape language.** Squares read as stable and heavy, triangles as dangerous and aggressive,
  circles as friendly. A set reads as one thing when one motif repeats through its pieces.
- **Detail in one place.** Each piece wants one bold feature (a crest, a tail, a beak, a brim).
  Our five-feature full helm read as "busy"; the plain shell read as "boring".
- **Contrast in brightness, not shape.** In one flat colour a boss facing the camera vanishes (the
  visor pivots did, until they were made dark). Two tones of the metal, plus a dark for openings,
  carry a piece.
- **Plate reads through overlap and edges.** A body painted with seams read as clothing; the same
  body cut into plates that overlap, with a turned edge on each, read as armour.

## 6. Where it might lead in R'th

Suggestions only; the owner decides. The powers already give each family a home:

- **Cruor's empire** (the biggest army, sworn soldiers): the great helm and the cuirass, square and
  heavy. The architecture's merlon could be its repeated motif (the "tower" sketch is a great helm
  crowned with one).
- **The dwarves of Frostpeak Forge** (the continent's great forge): close-fitting, heavily
  engineered helms, armets and close helmets, the frog-mouth's thick slab plates; squat and square.
- **Sere** (dry country, deep wells): what hot, dry armies wore: kettle hats, mail with plate, the
  chichak's conical, fluted skull and aventail; layered cloth over metal.
- **House Gall and the occult houses:** not plate wearers by style, but their soldiers or
  mercenaries could wear something strange: kawari kabuto-like oddities, masks.
- **Tiers.** A smithed set could grow with its tier, as the earlier band I-III sketches did (open
  helm, then a visor, then a crest), or each metal could keep one design and only change colour,
  as Old School does.

The five full-helm sketches (`HELM_DESIGNS` in `tools/art/blender/armour/platebody.py`) map onto
this: **tower** is a great helm with merlons, **drop** a sugarloaf with a band, **frostpeak** a
squat helm with a brow ledge, **sallet** the German sallet's tail, **frog-mouth** the Stechhelm's
beak. Shapes worth sketching next, from the tables above: the hounskull snout, the barbute's T
opening, the Corinthian's cheek pieces, the close helmet's stacked visor, a kabuto-style crest, the
Vendel spectacles.
