# Distillation

## Occultism, briefly

Occultism is one of three combat styles. It has three layers:

- **Phlegm** is the resource every style uses. Basic abilities build it, and stronger abilities spend it.
- **Bile** is a meter every player has, running from -100 (black bile) to +100 (yellow bile). Occult abilities push it in either direction, and some require a certain level. Sitting at a level also gives a passive bonus.
- **Spellbooks:** each occult house has its own, a themed set of spells. Casting a spell from a spellbook uses up **reagents**, a consumable fuel. Ordinary occult abilities use no reagents and cost only phlegm and may require to be within a certain bile range.

## What Distillation is

Distillation is how players make reagents. It is part of the Occultism skill, so doing it trains Occultism. It is a ritual-style activity:

1. The player collects the materials a rite calls for.
2. They go to a place where that rite is performed.
3. The rite runs for some time with little input, and the player checks in occasionally.
4. It produces reagents, which the player carries into combat.

The other combat styles have the same kind of supply: melee gets verses from the Religion skill, and ranged gets crafted ammunition.

## Decided

- Distillation is an activity that belongs to the Occultism skill, and is not a separate skill. Rites reward experience in Occultism in addition to item outputs.
- It is ritual-style and mostly hands-off once started.
- Its output is the reagents that spellbook spells consume.
- It centres on the occult houses.
- No house locks a player out. A player can learn from several.
- Progress comes from travelling to new houses and ritual sites, and of course training the skill.
- Specific sites create specific reagents. Much like specific runecrafting altars create specific runes

## Current direction (not final)

- Each house has its own rites, its own sites and its own spellbook. A house that favours burning would make yellow-leaning reagents, and one that favours dissolving would make black-leaning ones.
- Access to a house's harder rites is earned through trust or craft, and the rites need better materials and more demanding performances at higher tiers.
- **Corvin Sallow**, the friendly apothecary in the player's home town who turns out to be an occultist, teaches the first, crude but relatively safe rites.
- Materials could come from butchering creature carcasses (refined bile extracts), from gathering plants and ordinary materials, and from a dream-based skill that yields fragments for the highest grades.
